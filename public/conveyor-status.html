Work in progress — where it has got to

The run of work after 2.62. It starts by putting right what 2.62 left behind: a red check on production, wording in the records that should not be there, stale directions to a menu that moved, and a Share icon that draws as an empty box on the iPad. Then it answers what came back from the device the same day: red edges on metal under Aerochrome, a LUT list you have to leave to see anything, a top bar that wraps, and masks that cannot carry the controls you want inside them.

This page is updated in place. The link does not change.

The stages

  • Done Production's checks are green again. One word in the notes failed the check that looks for a person referred to by pronoun. It only runs on the server, so nothing local caught it, and 2.62 went out while it was red, because the deploy does not wait for the checks. Fixed; every check now passes on production's tree.
  • Done The records say what was wrong and what it measured, and nothing else. Some decision records and notes quoted the conversation they came from or named who asked. More than 330 places were rewritten as the engineering fact, and four details that were simply wrong were corrected in the same pass.
  • Answered Why Aerochrome draws red around steel against the sky. Not the metal's reflectance, for the colour outside it: one black post is blue on one edge and red on the other. Red and blue sit about half a pixel apart in the editing copy, the lens adds its own offset toward the corners, and Aerochrome's mixer turns that difference red at about twice its size. The round spots are Sky colour smoothing, and they go at 0. Then your own raw files answered it for your photograph. On NIR_3461, the frame you exported, the red on the wires and pylons is Aerochrome's foliage band: it picks by colour, and a thin dark wire against white cloud falls inside the foliage colours. With Foliage at 0, the strongly red pixels above the horizon go from 16,430 to none, the round spots go with them, and the grass loses its red too. Lining up the colour channels changes nothing on that frame. So the clean answer is Foliage applied only to the ground, which is the foliage-inside-a-mask you asked for and a stage of the mask work. Lowering Foliage cleans the metal today, at the cost of the grass.
  • Researched Masks that mask, with every control acting inside them. The model is Capture One's: pick a mask and the ordinary tabs follow it. No editor masks everything; geometry, the decode, the look choice and the LUT file stay whole-photo, with the reason shown in words. It can be built in stages, each its own release, and the first stage also fixes a tool aimed at a mask ignoring what that mask subtracts. The mask record is rewritten around this, with its stages and the three choices it leaves you; pictures of the panel come before any code.
  • Waiting on you The top bar and the start card. Measured: the bar needs about 1020 pixels and gets 736 on a landscape iPad, so it wraps; in portrait it takes four rows and leaves the photo 14% of the screen; the start card hides the practice photos below the fold. Shorter labels or icons do not fix it, because the bar carries three kinds of action at once. Three layouts were drawn in the real app and sent as pictures; each gives a one-row bar at every size. A check that holds the bar to one row is in the repository and fails today, as it should. The side panel that folds away stays a separate question.
  • Waiting on you LUTs you tap through on your own photo. Comparing eighteen LUTs today means eighteen round trips through a full-screen list. The usual shape is a browser beside the photo, each tile drawn from your picture, None first, tapping the chosen one again for its strength, and import, share and delete kept apart. Browsing must not fill the undo history, and a LUT must survive the iPad closing the tab. The record is written, and four layouts were drawn in the real app with tiles made from a real photograph and sent as pictures. It is built once you pick one.
  • On production One build of small fixes, promoted on your go. The Share icon on the start page, which draws a character the iPad has no font for, and its button is now a full 44-pixel target; the messages that still sent you to "Profiles & LUTs"; the licence text the four fonts require; and a paragraph saying where the app's numbers come from. The comments in the shipped pages no longer credit anyone, proven to change nothing else in the build. Production serves it as v2.62.14, checked against the live site.
  • Done The checks that should have caught the red. On the hub: seven lessons carry the day's evidence, the lessons check reads every enforcement line (it was missing 23), and the doctrine sync names each lesson that changed. Here: a decision record that says who asked, or that it was asked in a conversation, is refused; the release notes check counts what the page actually shows (5, where it had been reporting 12); and the shared checks run from the newest hub. Only the privacy checks before a commit are left, and that waits on your answer.
  • Done The build and deploy workflows are locked down. Every action they run is pinned to a checked commit, the deploy can read the repository and nothing more, and a value typed into the asset workflow is treated as text, never as a command. The security scanner went from 20 findings to 1; the one left is written down with why. The first deploy on the new setup was read step by step and went out.
  • Recorded Two things about looks. A look cannot be taken off: no look button removes it, Reset brings it back if the photograph opened wearing it, and the only way out is a setting called Default look. And a look saved as a file is expected to be greyed out in Files on the iPad, the same way the colour files were. Both have records with a remedy and a rank.
  • On staging The export edges, and a measurement for the mask work. Every full-size export had a line of the wrong colour along all four edges, one pixel wide, not just the top and left: the export read a neighbouring pixel of the wrong colour at the border. Fixed and measured on a practice photograph: every edge now matches the pixels beside it, and nothing further in changes. The device test page also gains a row saying how much room the editor's graphics have for masks with their own settings, which decides how the mask work is built on your iPad. Every check passed on it: the export check, exports across cores against one core, every view agreeing, and the accessibility sweep, and the export check and sweep again once it sat on top of v2.62.14. Your pass came back good: your export's four edges are clean, and your iPad has room for about 46 masks' own settings in the editor's graphics (148 of 1,024 rows used), so masks carry their values directly. Waiting for your go to put it on production.
  • Built, held Masks, stage one. A "here only" tool now stays out of an area subtracted from its mask: rendered first, the tool reached the subtracted area while the mask's own adjustment did not, and the check written for it failed on the old build before it passed on the new one. A mask can now add to the one above it, "Add to it", so a Colour mask can finish a sky the automatic selection missed; a check holds all three joins on every commit. The device test page times a frame with eight masks. Every walk passed on it. It goes to staging after v2.62.16 reaches production.
  • Next Masks, stage two and after. The ordinary controls act inside a selected mask, in stages, each one its own device pass, once you have answered what a mask's value means and where the mask list lives. Foliage limited to a mask is the stage that fixes the red wires, pylons and cladding: on your building, Foliage at 0 takes the red on the cladding from 13,561 pixels to none and the red on the grass from 87,991 to none, and the grass needs it while the building does not.

Found, and not fixed

Things measured on the way through that are real and are not yet repaired. They are written down so they are not re-discovered as new.

  • A mask joined to the one above still shows its own Brightness, Contrast and Saturation sliders, which nothing reads once it is joined: the adjustment above is the one that applies. Read in the code, not yet seen on screen. Owned by the mask work's second stage, where the controls follow the selected mask.
  • Foliage can only be set for the whole photo. It selects a colour, not a place, so bright cladding on a building reddens along with the grass. Owned by the mask work.
  • Full view shows on the start screen and does nothing there, and a pressed Histogram is drawn exactly like the Open button. Owned by the top-bar work.
  • A LUT does not survive the iPad closing the tab. The rest of the edit comes back and the LUT does not. Help says so; the LUT work changes it, using the way a saved look already keeps its LUT.
