Session work — where it has got to

Moving between photos in a long session takes seconds, and exporting a keeper takes over the screen while it runs. The work below treats those as one thing: the session is built to hold one photo set up for editing, and it is used to pass a few hundred through. Everything the passing-through needs already exists in the app, in the tools that keep nothing — the deciding keys are in Quick look, unattended work is in Batch, and a non-blocking export is in Macro Studio. Most of what follows moves those in, rather than inventing anything.

The stages

  • Shipped to staging The deciding keys work the moment Quick look opens. P, X, U and C were correct from the day they landed and could not be reached: they listen on the grid, and the grid never had the keyboard until a photo had been tapped — which is itself the pick.
  • Building now The switch says where its seconds went, and this page exists. The version report gains a Last switch line whose parts add up to the whole, and an Edits held in memory count. The test page gains a measurement of what it costs to find one photo's pieces in a big session. Nothing the app does changes.
  • Waiting on you The photo you are opening jumps the decode queue. Only if your three reports say the wait is the queue. If they say something else, this is dropped and the something else is what gets worked on.
  • Not started A default look, set once. Every photo opens wearing it, in every session, until you change it.
  • Not started Pick and reject on the photo you are looking at. The same two words and the same two keys Quick look already uses, on the session, surviving a reload, and reachable by finger as well as by key.
  • Not started Deciding lets the app put the photo down. Marking a photo you are finished with frees what it is holding. Coming back reopens it from the saved copy, which the app says rather than leaves you to discover.
  • Not started Export stops owning the screen. The same export, the same two presses, with the editor still live while it runs.
  • Not started Everything you exported saves in one go. Finished exports collect until you ask for them, as one file or one zip.

Found, and not fixed

Things measured on the way through that are real and are not being repaired by this work. They are written down so they are not re-discovered as new.

  • Finding a photo's pieces is not what makes a big session slow, on the machine this was measured on. Picking 850 stored pieces out of 144,500 took 9.2 ms against 6.8 ms out of an empty store. Worth re-running on your own device from the test page, because that is the one that matters.
  • A Quick look preview does not record which look it was rendered under. Open the grid while a photo is showing under a look and the stored picture wears that look, under a key that says nothing about it. The escape is the grid's own Build these again. The repair costs every stored preview on the device once, so it is its own piece of work.
  • Turning Restore depth on or off does not redraw the strip. Every tile then shows a claim about the photo that is no longer true, until something else redraws it.
  • The strip is rebuilt on every thumbnail that lands, and each rebuild forces the browser to lay the row out twice. On a long set that is a few hundred layouts nobody asked for. Now measured rather than assumed — it was 2 ms per switch on a six-photo set here, and the number that matters is the one from a long session.
  • An edited photo is never let go of. Every photo visited in a session keeps its full working state, including the ones you are finished with. That is what the fifth stage is for; until then, the count is at least visible in the report.
  • A photo that has been healed, stickered or warped exports on one thread. The parallel export refuses those, so they are as slow as they ever were.
  • The accessibility walk still lives outside the repository. It is rebuilt and run before each of these goes to staging, and its result is written into the notes, but nothing in the repository holds it.

Everything here happens on your device. This page is a record of work in progress and carries nothing about your photographs.
