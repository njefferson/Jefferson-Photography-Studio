Session work — where it has got to

Moving between photos in a long session takes seconds, and exporting a keeper takes over the screen while it runs. The work below treats those as one thing: the session is built to hold one photo set up for editing, and it is used to pass a few hundred through. Everything the passing-through needs already exists in the app, in the tools that keep nothing — the deciding keys are in Quick look, unattended work is in Batch, and a non-blocking export is in Macro Studio. Most of what follows moves those in, rather than inventing anything.

The stages

  • Shipped to staging The deciding keys work the moment Quick look opens. P, X, U and C were correct from the day they landed and could not be reached: they listen on the grid, and the grid never had the keyboard until a photo had been tapped — which is itself the pick.
  • Shipped to staging The switch says where its seconds went, and this page exists. The version report gains a Last switch line whose parts add up to the whole, and an Edits held in memory count. The test page gains a measurement of what it costs to find one photo's pieces in a big session. Nothing the app does changes.
  • Shipped to staging The photo you are opening jumps the decode queue. Built, because the report from the device was the evidence it was waiting for: moving between photos is slow specifically while the strip is filling in, and Quick look — which has the decoders to itself — is fine. The photo you tapped now goes to the head of the queue instead of behind the pictures being built for photos you have not asked for.
  • Shipped to staging A default look, set once. In Settings, under the colour pickers. Every photo opens wearing it, in every session, until you change it. Pressing a look still changes the set you are in; Reset keeps it; Hold: Untouched still shows the bare file. Part one of three in the next capability release.
  • Shipped to staging Pick and reject on the photo you are looking at. The same two words and the same two keys Quick look already uses, on the session: two buttons in the strip's own bar, P and X and U from the keyboard, the verdict written on the tile in a word, counted in the strip's line, and still there after a reload. A pick made in Quick look now arrives in the session as a pick. Part two of three in the next capability release.
  • Shipped to staging Deciding lets the app put the photo down. Marking a photo you are finished with frees what it was holding, once the saved copy is really on the device. Walking a six-photo set and deciding on each holds two instead of six. Coming back rebuilds it from the saved copy: the picture, the look and every slider return, the undo history does not, and the tile says so before you press anything. A photo with brush masks, an imported LUT or a warp is kept instead, and the strip says which and why. This one carries the release: v2.44.
  • Shipped to staging Export stops owning the screen. The same export and the same two presses, with the editor live while it runs: move to another photo, grade it, drag a slider — the file being written is the one you pressed Export on, proven byte for byte against the old flow. Progress and the finished file sit on a line over the photo, so they are reachable whichever panel tab you are on. A failure explains itself, offers Try again, and is remembered in the report.
  • Shipped to staging Every screen is measured now, and six buttons were too small. The accessibility sweep was rebuilt from nothing before each release and lived nowhere; it is in the repository now, and its list of screens is a rule rather than a note. It had been opening three of the fifteen dialogs in the editor and checking one of the seven pages. Given all of them it found six buttons at 35 px — on the dialog that asks what happens to the location written into your photograph, and on the one that asks you to confirm — against the 44 px a finger needs. Every dialog button clears it now, and every screen is checked on every run.
  • Shipped to staging Four ways a tile lied about the photograph. The tiles are how you decide, so a tile is a promise about what tapping it will show. Four things broke that promise and all four are fixed together. A quick look kept its rendered tiles on the device and handed them back under a different look, saying so in its own words. Opening a photograph flattened its own tile. Restore depth changed what every tile in the strip would show and redrew none of them. And a look pressed on the photograph you were looking at reached every tile except that one. Each is a picture that was wrong rather than code that was, which is why green gates never saw them; the walk that does now lives in the repo.
  • Shipped to staging Export the photos you picked. The Export panel says how many are picked and exports them all in one press, into the same collection that hands over as one file or one zip. Each goes through the app's own open and the same export the button runs, proven byte for byte — including a photo picked in Quick look and never opened. Stoppable, and it puts you back on the photo you were on. With it: a quarter-turn now survives leaving a photo, and straightening gained a tenth-of-a-degree button either side of the slider, which measures 0.429° per pixel of drag. v2.46.
  • Shipped to staging Everything you exported saves in one go. Every finished export is kept until you ask for it — one press hands over the file itself when there is one, a zip when there are more. They survive closing the tab, and they are kept apart from Batch, so saving a batch cannot sweep a morning's keepers. This one carries v2.45.

Found, and not fixed

Things measured on the way through that are real and are not being repaired by this work. They are written down so they are not re-discovered as new.

  • Finding a photo's pieces is not what makes a big session slow, on the machine this was measured on. Picking 850 stored pieces out of 144,500 took 9.2 ms against 6.8 ms out of an empty store. Worth re-running on your own device from the test page, because that is the one that matters.
  • The strip is rebuilt on every thumbnail that lands, and each rebuild forces the browser to lay the row out twice. On a long set that is a few hundred layouts nobody asked for. Now measured rather than assumed — it was 2 ms per switch on a six-photo set here, and the number that matters is the one from a long session.
  • An UNDECIDED photo is still never let go of. Releasing is tied to a verdict, so a session walked without deciding holds every photo it has visited, exactly as before. The count is visible in the report.
  • A photo that has been healed, stickered or warped exports on one thread, and the sliders stutter while it does. The parallel export refuses those. Measured here: 15.1 s for a 5.2 megapixel frame, 4.9 s of it paused to let the screen keep up, against 6.6 s on three threads.
  • A verdict pressed at the instant the page reloads can be lost. The mark is written to storage durably, and a reload can beat the write: the one lost is always the last one pressed. It matters more than it sounds, because an iPad drops background tabs and reloads them by itself, which is what a long culling session looks like. Repairing it is a decision about when to write, not a patch.

Everything here happens on your device. This page is a record of work in progress and carries nothing about your photographs.
