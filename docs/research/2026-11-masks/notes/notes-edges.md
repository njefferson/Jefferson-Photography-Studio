## What the sources say

What each takes as input
- HQ-SAM takes what SAM takes, a prompt of points, a box or a coarse mask, and the picture, and it does not take SAM's finished mask as input: it reuses SAM's encoder and decoder and predicts a new mask directly, "instead of taking the coarse mask and images as the input and feeding it into a separate refinement network" (1, section 2). A new learnable High-Quality Output Token of size 1 by 256 joins SAM's four mask tokens and the prompt tokens in the decoder, and its three-layer MLP gives the kernels for a point-wise product with fused high-quality features (1, sections 3.2.1 and 7). The fused features are three: the early-layer encoder feature after the first global attention block (the 6th block output of 24 for ViT-Large), the final-layer encoder feature, both 64 by 64, and the decoder's mask feature of 256 by 256; the first two are raised to 256 by 256 by transposed convolutions and the three are summed after simple convolutions (1, section 3.2.2 and 7).
- CascadePSP takes the picture and an initial mask from any method, and, inside the cascade, "multiple imperfect segmentation masks at different scales": the lower-resolution masks are bilinearly upsampled to one size and concatenated with the RGB picture (2, section 3.1). The first level starts with the input mask copied into each input channel, and after each level one channel is replaced by the upsampled output of that level (2, section 3.2.1).
- SegFix takes only the picture to build its maps, "without exploring the segmentation result" (3, section 1). It predicts a boundary map and a direction map (eight discrete directions by default), turns the direction map into an offset map, and then any model's label map is refined by replacing the label of each boundary pixel with the label of the interior pixel the offset points to (3, sections 3.1 and 3.2). The offset map is made once for a picture and applies to the output of any model (3, section 3.1).
- ViTMatte takes the picture and a trimap, a grayscale hint with 0 for background, 1 for foreground and 0.5 for unknown, joined channel-wise into four channels, and gives an alpha matte at the input's resolution (4, sections 3.1 and 3.2). The page describes a trimap as a manually drawn hint map (4, Figure 3 caption); for training it makes trimaps by dilation and erosion with a random kernel size in 1 to 30 (4, section 4.2).
- The guided filter takes two pictures and gives one: a guidance image I and a filtering input p, "both given beforehand according to the application", which "can be identical", and the output q (6a, section 3; 6b, section 3). It takes two parameters: a window radius r and a regularisation parameter ε (6b, Algorithm 1 and section 3.1; 6a, section 3.1). The guidance may be one channel or colour: a colour guide uses a 3 by 3 covariance matrix, and a multichannel input is filtered channel by channel (6b, section 3.5; 6a, section 3.6). The model is that q is a linear transform of I in each square window of radius r, so "q has an edge only if I has an edge" (6b, section 3.1). Its structure-transferring use: q is locally a scaling of I plus an offset, so structure from the guide reaches the output "even if the filtering input p is smooth" (6b, section 3.6). The paper's own mask use, "guided feathering", takes a binary mask from "graph-cut or other segmentation methods" as p and the colour picture as I, and gives an alpha matte near the object boundary (6b, section 3.6 and Figure 10, section 5 and Figure 15; 6a, section 4 and Figure 9). The input pictures in its figures are scaled to the range 0 to 1 (6b, Figure 2 caption).

At what resolution each works and whether it reads the full-size picture
- HQ-SAM works on SAM's grid: encoder features 64 by 64, a corrected mask at 256 by 256 made by summing the logits of SAM's output token and the new token, then upsampled to 1,024 by 1,024 as the output (1, sections 3.1 and 3.3). The paper does not describe a pass at a larger size.
- CascadePSP works at three output strides, 8, 4 and 1, in one module (2, Figure 2 caption). Its Global step downsamples the picture so the long side is L and refines the whole picture; its Local step takes crops of L by L at the full resolution from the Global step's two last outputs resized to the original size, chips 16 pixels from each side of the crop output, steps by L over 2 minus 32, and averages the overlapping outputs (2, sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.2). L is 900 in the paper's experiments. Both steps are used for a picture whose longer side is 900 or more and only the Global step below that (2, section 3.2.4). The paper says it needs no high-resolution training: training uses random 224 by 224 crops (2, section 3.3).
- SegFix works at full resolution by design: its backbone is a high-resolution network with an extra branch so that the output maps are at 2 times the usual feature size (section 3.2: "HRNet-2×"), and it applies "full-resolution boundary maps and direction maps to refine full-resolution coarse label maps" (3, section 3.2). The boundary it predicts is a band: ground-truth pixels within γ of another class's pixel, γ set to 5 (3, section 3.3 and Table 3 caption).
- ViTMatte's transformer gives one feature map at stride 16; a lightweight convolution stream gives detail maps at one half, one quarter and one eighth of the input, and the fusion brings the result back to the input size (4, sections 3.2 and 3.4). It trains on 512 by 512 crops (4, section 3.5). Its alpha is predicted for the whole picture, but the paper's headline scores are taken only inside the trimap's unknown region (4, section 4.3).
- The guided filter works on the pixel grid of the pictures it is given, over a square window of radius r, and its cost does not depend on r (6b, sections 3.1 and 4). Its own two-scale use is joint upsampling: the coefficients are computed at the coarse scale from the coarse guide and input, bilinearly upsampled to the fine scale, and the output is q = aI + b with the fine-scale guide; the paper reports this at "about 20 ms/Mp" against 180 ms per megapixel for joint bilateral upsampling, and calls the result "visually comparable" (6b, section 5 and Figure 17; 6a, section 4 and Figure 11). The mask examples in the paper run at r = 60 with ε = 10 to the minus 6 (6a, Figure 9) and ε = 0.01 squared (6b, Figure 10); a 6-megapixel picture is the paper's size for feathering (6a, section 4).

What each adds
- HQ-SAM: one learnable token, its three-layer MLP and three convolutions, with the whole of SAM frozen (1, section 3.3). The paper reports that fine-tuning SAM's decoder or adding "a new decoder module severely degrades the general zero-shot segmentation performance" and that training the whole SAM collapsed results (Table 4: 38.0 mIoU and 12.2 mBIoU on the four detailed datasets, 0.2 boundary AP on COCO, against 79.5, 71.1 and 33.3 for SAM) (1, section 1 and Table 4).
- CascadePSP: a refinement network (PSPNet with a ResNet-50 backbone, skip connections, two ResNet blocks per upsampling stage) run as a cascade (2, sections 3.1 and 4.2), trained class-agnostically on 36,572 pictures from four saliency and few-shot sets with the input masks made by perturbing the ground truth (subsampling the contour, then random dilation and erosion) and not by running any model (2, section 3.3). Its loss includes an L1 term on the gradient of the mask, weighted 5 (2, section 3.1).
- SegFix: a boundary branch and a direction branch, each a 1 by 1 convolution, batch norm and ReLU on the backbone's features, with a linear classifier (3, section 3.2). It adds no change to the segmentation model.
- ViTMatte: a hybrid attention (global attention in the last block of each group, window attention elsewhere), a residual convolution neck after each group, and a detail capture module of fewer than 3 million parameters (4, sections 1, 3.3 and 3.4). It starts from a pretrained plain transformer (DINO weights for the small form, MAE for the base) (4, section 4.2).

What each reports for edge quality, by a named measure, against what
- HQ-SAM: mask IoU (mIoU) and boundary IoU (mBIoU), plus boundary AP, with a stricter boundary AP made by changing "the default dilation ratio from 0.02 to 0.01" (1, section 4.1). On four fine-grained datasets with boxes made from the ground-truth masks, SAM scores 79.5 mIoU and 71.1 mBIoU and HQ-SAM 89.1 and 81.8 (1, Table 2); on the first of them SAM's mBIoU goes from 52.8 to 70.4 (1, section 4.2). On a high-resolution set with a coarse mask as the prompt, SAM scores 66.6 mIoU and 41.8 mBIoU and HQ-SAM 86.9 and 75.1 (1, Table 6). With a box prompt on the same set the scores are 81.1 and 70.4 for SAM and 86.0 and 75.3 for HQ-SAM (1, Table 6). On COCO with a detector's boxes the boundary AP goes from 33.3 to 34.4 (1, Table 7).
- CascadePSP: IoU and its own mean Boundary Accuracy (mBA), the segmentation accuracy within five radii of the ground-truth boundary, averaged; the radii run from 3 pixels up to a bound written from the width plus the height and the number 300 (the text form loses the fraction's layout) (2, section 3.1). Refining DeepLabV3+ on a relabelled PASCAL set moves IoU from 87.13 to 89.01 and mBA from 61.68 to 72.10 (2, Table 1); on its own high-resolution set (images from 2048 by 1600 to 5000 by 3600, 50 validation and 100 test objects) it moves 89.42 to 92.23 IoU and 60.25 to 74.59 mBA for the same input model (2, sections 3.2.4 and 4.1, Table 3). The two steps separately on the validation set: Global only 91.86 IoU and 73.10 mBA, Local only 91.35 and 73.06, both 92.01 and 75.59, from 89.65 and 60.94 (2, Table 2).
- SegFix: mIoU and boundary F-score; the F-score thresholds of 0.0003, 0.0006 and 0.0009 are said to correspond to 1, 2 and 3 pixels (3, section 4.1). With DeepLabv3 as the base (79.5 mIoU, 56.6 F-score at 1 pixel) SegFix adds 1.0 mIoU and 4.4 F-score with the largest backbone (3, Table 2 caption and rows). Ground-truth offsets, as an upper bound, add 3.1 mIoU and 12.0 F-score (3, Table 1). On the page's per-class boundary F-score at 1 pixel, DeepLabv3's vegetation goes from 49.5 to 54.7 and sky from 60.5 to 64.7, mean 56.6 to 61.0 (3, Table 8 rows); per-class mIoU for vegetation goes from 92.8 to 93.2 and sky from 95.0 to 95.3 (3, Table 13 rows). The datasets are street scenes (Cityscapes, GTA5) and ADE20K (3, section 4.1).
- ViTMatte: SAD, MSE, Grad and Conn against a ground-truth alpha, lower being better, on composited test sets: Composition-1k SAD 21.46 for the small form and 20.33 for the base, scored only in the trimap's unknown region; Distinctions-646 SAD 21.22 and 17.05, scored over the whole picture (4, sections 4.1 and 4.3, Tables 1 and 2). Its trimaps for Distinctions-646 are generated by erosion by the authors since no official ones exist (4, section 4.3).
- The guided filter: the papers report no number for edge quality against a ground truth on the mask use. The mask result is compared by eye with Photoshop's Refine Edge and with closed-form matting on a short hair case: "visually comparable" with the closed-form solution, "fast feedback (<1s)" for a 6-megapixel picture, against about two minutes for closed-form matting (6a, section 4 and Figure 9; 6b, section 5 and Figure 15). The journal paper adds that, combined with a global sampling method, it is "the best performed filtering-based matting method in the alphamatting benchmark" as of June 2012 (6b, section 5). Its one printed number comparing outputs is a "PSNR" between the guided filter's and the bilateral filter's results at corresponding parameters, 37.09 to 39.38 dB, which the paper says is analogous to measures used in bilateral-filter approximation and is not a score against a ground truth (6b, Figure 2 and its footnote 2). Its edge claim is stated as: "edge-preserving smoothing property like the bilateral filter, but does not suffer from the gradient reversal artifacts" (6a, section 1); in the self-guided case (I the same as p) the patch-wise model is "guaranteed to not have gradient reversal", and "in practice, we do not observe the gradient reversal artifacts in all experiments" (6b, section 3.4); the gradient of the output is approximately a-bar times the gradient of the guide, "meaning that abrupt intensity changes in I can be mostly maintained in q" (6b, section 3.1). What ε does: windows whose variance is much smaller than ε are smoothed and those much larger are preserved; "the effect of ε in the guided filter is similar to the range variance in the bilateral filter" (6b, section 3.2; 6a, section 3.2).

What each costs
- HQ-SAM: fewer than 0.5 percent more parameters; for the large form 5.1 million learnable parameters against 1,191 for SAM, 4.8 frames per second against 5.0, and 7.6G of memory for both; trained in 4 hours on 8 graphics cards (1, section 1 and Table 1). The paper says it "shares the heavy ViT encoder of SAM, and thus cannot achieve a real-time speed", and proposes Light HQ-SAM on MobileSAM's tiny encoder: 41.2 frames per second, 1.7 added to the model's size (printed in MB), COCO AP 44.3 to 45.0 (1, section 4.3 and Table 10). The page does not say what hardware the frame rates were taken on.
- CascadePSP: L of 900 uses 3.16 GB of graphics memory, L of 500 uses 1.16 GB at a cost of 0.6 mBA; the memory depends on L and not on the picture size because fusion runs on the CPU (2, section 3.2.3). The whole refinement takes "about 6.6s" for its Figure 1 (3492 by 2328), with the Local step run only in the region of interest; it trained about 16 hours on two 1080Ti cards (2, section 4.2). The page gives no parameter count in the text read.
- SegFix: at 2048 by 1024 on a single V100 (PyTorch 1.4 with TensorRT) the runtimes are 16 ms with 9.6 million parameters for the smallest backbone, 20 ms with 29.4 million, 69 ms with 47.3 million for the largest; the page says it "reaches real-time speed with light-weight backbone" (3, Table 2 and its caption). The smaller backbones add 0.8 and 0.9 mIoU and 3.7 and 3.9 F-score against 1.0 and 4.4 (3, Table 2).
- ViTMatte: 25.8 million parameters for the small form against 27.9 and 44.8 million for two earlier matting methods (4, Table 3); at 2048 by 2048 the paper's hybrid attention with four global layers takes 0.50 of the full global attention's cost, which is 3.28T for the plain 12-layer form (4, Table 4); a grid-sampled inference pass cut memory on a 2048 by 2048 picture from 20.3G to 6.8G for a SAD of 21.54 against 21.46 (4, Table 8). The page gives no time in seconds or milliseconds.
- The guided filter: linear time in the number of pixels, "independent of the window radius r and the intensity range", exact and not approximate, because every sum in it is a box filter done by an integral image or a moving sum (6b, abstract and section 4; 6a, section 3.6). The journal paper's machine is a single-core 3.0 GHz Intel Core i7 with 8 GB, C++, no SIMD instructions; a mean filter takes about 5 to 7 ms per megapixel there. Grey-scale smoothing takes 40 ms per megapixel (4 mean filters), colour smoothing with I the same as p 150 ms per megapixel (18 mean filters), and a colour guide with a one-channel input, the feathering case, 140 ms per megapixel (17 mean filters) (6b, section 4, Table 1 and Table 3). The earlier paper gives 80 ms per megapixel for grey-scale and about 0.3 s per megapixel for a colour guide on a 2.0 GHz Core 2 Duo laptop (6a, section 3.6). Its comparisons: the O(N) bilateral filter at 155 ms per megapixel for 32 bins and 310 for 64 bins as reported by that method's author, and Domain Transform at 160 and 60 ms per megapixel for colour (6b, Tables 2 and 3). No memory figure is given.

Which is a model needing weights and a runtime, and which is a filter
- HQ-SAM, CascadePSP, SegFix and ViTMatte are each learned networks needing trained weights and a runtime: HQ-SAM's weights sit on top of SAM's (1, section 3.3), CascadePSP is a ResNet-50 PSPNet (2, section 4.2), SegFix a high-resolution network (3, section 3.2), ViTMatte a pretrained plain transformer with convolutions (4, section 3.2). The papers give addresses of code and weights (1 and 2, 3 and 4, in their abstracts or footnotes); those pages were not asked, so their licences and file sizes are not known from these pages. None of the four pages states a licence for its weights.
- The pages name filters only as what they are set against: the two refinement papers say graphical-model refinement (a conditional random field, region growing) "adhere to low-level color boundaries without fully utilizing high-level semantic information" and "cannot fix large error regions" (1, section 2; 2, section 2), and SegFix compares itself with DenseCRF: with DeepLabv3 as the base, F-score 56.6, DenseCRF 60.9, SegFix 61.0, both 64.1, and mIoU 79.5, 79.7, 80.5 and 80.5 (3, Table 5). The guided filter is the one of the five that is not a learned network: it has no weights and nothing to train, and is defined by its two equations and the five-line procedure of Algorithm 1 (6b, section 3.1 and Algorithm 1). The journal paper says it "provided a public code" in Matlab (6b, section 1 and reference 27); that page was not asked and no licence is known from these pages. The paper sets it against the bilateral filter, the joint bilateral filter, Domain Transform and the weighted least squares filter, and against closed-form matting and Photoshop's Refine Edge on the mask use (6b, sections 5 and Figures 10, 15 and 18), and describes it as being "closely related to the matting Laplacian matrix": one Jacobi iteration of the matting problem (6b, section 3.7).

What each says of its own failure cases
- HQ-SAM: two failure cases are shown, "extremely dark environments or very tiny metal rods", where it improves on SAM and still cannot give a fully correct mask (1, Figure 12 caption and section 9). It is also said not to be real time with the full encoder (1, section 4.3).
- CascadePSP: with a large input error the output "adheres well to the color boundary" and so is wrong, "due to the lack of semantic information"; the paper says its method "relies on the input segmentation and low-level cues" and cannot correct a missing part (such as a table leg or a hand) that super-resolution of the mask would not give either (2, section 4.3 and Figure 11 caption). For a scene parsed into many classes it refines each connected component alone, with 25 percent padding, and falls back to the original label where all confidences are under 0.5; for "stuff" classes it lowers the outputs because they are "not strong in objectness", and the gains there are 0.73 to 0.99 mIoU and 1.07 to 2.00 mBA (2, section 4.4 and Table 4).
- SegFix: its reliability rests on interior predictions being more reliable than boundary ones, and "the segmentation performance ceiling of our approach is also determined by the interior pixels' prediction accuracy" (3, section 3.3 discussion); "there still exist some errors located in the interior regions that our approach fail to address as we are mainly focused on the thin boundary refinement" (3, section 7.5). Most errors lie within about 5 pixels of the boundary (3, Figure 2 caption). The gain is largest for small objects (poles, traffic lights, signs) (3, section 7.1).
- ViTMatte: the page shows no failure case. The page says it works from a trimap that "provides sufficient global semantic information" (4, section 5.1) and that the trimap is hand-drawn or generated.
- The guided filter: the journal paper's Limitations paragraph says it has "a common limitation of other explicit filters": "it may exhibit halos near some edges", halos being unwanted smoothing of an edge, and that they are "unavoidable for local filters when the filters are forced to smooth some edges", for example where strong textures are smoothed and the weaker edges are smoothed with them; a global optimisation filter distributes the blurring and suppresses the halos "at the price of global intensity shifting" (6b, section 5 and Figure 18). The ECCV paper says it is "not directly applicable for sparse inputs like strokes" and that "it is ambiguous for a low-level and local operator to determine which edge should be smoothed and which should be preserved" (6a, section 5). A grey-scale guide gives halos where the edge is not distinguishable in grey, which a colour guide does not (6b, section 3.5 and Figure 8). The kernel with a box window is rotationally asymmetric and slightly biased to the x and y axes; a Gaussian-weighted variant removes this and the paper still uses the box form in all its experiments because it finds it "always good enough" (6b, section 3.4 and Figure 4). Larger ε and larger r blur more in the paper's cat figures (6a, Figure 1; 6b, Figure 2).

## What none of the pages says

- The guided filter on a graded input: the papers' mask input is binary, and no page says what the filter does to a soft weight, a mask already graded, or how r and ε should follow the picture's size; the examples are given at fixed values with the picture sizes mostly unstated (the feathering picture is called 6 megapixels, the haze picture 600 by 400).
- The guided filter with a one-channel lightness guide on a photograph of the app's kind: the papers' guides are ordinary colour or grey photographs, HDR and flash pictures; none is near-infrared or false colour, and the only page on a grey-scale guide says it gives halos where the edge is not distinguishable in grey (6b, Figure 8).
- The guided filter's edge quality by a boundary measure at a pixel tolerance: no page prints one, the mask comparisons are by eye, and a score at four pixels is not given for the filter either.
- The guided filter's time on a tablet, in a browser, on WebAssembly or on one thread of a phone: the times are for single-core C++ on a 3.0 GHz desktop processor and a 2.0 GHz laptop one, and no page converts them.
- Any of the four methods on a photograph of the kind this app opens: the pages' test sets are street scenes, everyday objects, salient objects, composited foreground images and a set labelled by hand for PASCAL classes; none is near-infrared or false colour, and none is a landscape selection of sky or foliage as a region.
- Any time of the four on a tablet, or on a device with WebAssembly and one thread: the pages give times on graphics cards (SegFix on a V100, CascadePSP on its own card with seconds for one picture, HQ-SAM as frames per second without a card named) and no page measures any of the four in a browser.
- File sizes and licences of the four's weights.
- A threshold for a graded mask: the papers score masks (HQ-SAM and CascadePSP), labels (SegFix) or an alpha (ViTMatte), and the pages read do not say how any of the four takes a graded input such as the soft weight this app makes, nor how a score is taken on a graded mask.
- Whether the HQ-SAM token or the full encoder's intermediate features are exposed by any file a browser can run: the page describes them inside the network and says nothing of an exported form.
- How a mask refined at one picture's edges is carried to another photograph: none of the four pages touches it.
- Any score at four pixels: the boundary pixel tolerances the pages print are 1, 2 and 3 pixels (SegFix) and five radii from 3 pixels (CascadePSP); HQ-SAM's tolerance is a dilation ratio (0.02 and 0.01) whose reference length the text does not give.

## What this bears on, without choosing

- Regions (one thing per click joined by name). The four methods each work per object or per prompt: HQ-SAM per prompt, CascadePSP per object with its fusion rule for many objects (the original label wherever no refined confidence reaches 0.5), SegFix on a whole label map, class-agnostic, ViTMatte per object from a trimap. None says how an edge is made where two regions meet, other than CascadePSP's fusion rule and SegFix's labels.
- Edges (the cue stage at full size in a narrower band). Two of the four are built around a narrow band: SegFix predicts a band within 5 pixels of the boundary and replaces labels there only, and ViTMatte scores and trains its alpha in the unknown band of a trimap made by dilation and erosion of 1 to 30 pixels; CascadePSP's Local step works over the whole picture at full size in crops, and HQ-SAM does not read the picture beyond SAM's 1,024. What this reading cannot say is whether a band cut from a graded mask can feed the cue stage at the sizes the app has; the pages give no such test. The guided filter's own two-scale use is the one place a page works with a coarse input and a full-size guide: coefficients taken at the coarse scale, bilinearly upsampled, applied to the full-size guide, about 20 ms per megapixel, "visually comparable" to joint bilateral upsampling (6b, section 5); the papers do not apply it to a band only.
- Carrying a mask to another photograph. No page read bears on it; the reading is silent here and the handoff's other note on carrying is the place for it.
- The editor's own mask. Of the five, only the guided filter is already in the app (its parameters were read from `src/samplemask.ts`: the lightness as the guide, a radius of 12 at a grid side of 1,024, a regularisation of 0.005). What the paper says of it, set beside that: it names an input and a guide, the output has an edge only where the guide has one, and the ε it prints for pictures scaled 0 to 1 run from 10 to the minus 6 to 0.4 squared (6a, Figures 1 and 9; 6b, Figure 2); its one-channel guide gives halos where an edge is not distinguishable in grey, and a colour guide is the paper's remedy (6b, section 3.5 and Figure 8); and its mask use takes a binary input and a colour guide (6b, Figure 10). The pages do not say whether a lightness guide made from a near-infrared photograph meets that limit.
- Record 111's bar (no wrong region over a hundredth of the frame, edges within four pixels, judged over the whole photograph). HQ-SAM's and CascadePSP's papers report that their gains come from boundary detail and from fixing broken holes (1, section 4.3; 2, section 3.2.4), and each reports boundary measures by tolerance or ratio, none at four pixels; SegFix states that interior errors are left alone, so a wrong region of a hundredth of the frame is outside its reach; CascadePSP's own failure case is a large wrong region it cannot remove; ViTMatte needs the region to be right already, since the trimap fixes what is foreground. The measure that reads closest to the bar is SegFix's boundary F-score at 1, 2 and 3 pixels and CascadePSP's mBA, both over whole pictures of their test sets, none a photograph of the app's kind. The guided filter's papers print no boundary measure; its output is a weighted average of the input (6b, equation 1), so a region wrong in the input is what is averaged, and the papers do not discuss an input whose region is wrong.
- The device's figures from record 112 (an encode of a 1,024-pixel picture in about 2.4 s and a decode in about 0.12 s on one WebAssembly thread; WebGPU offered and unmeasured; 4 cores and no threads without isolation headers; 60 MB of files kept on the device). HQ-SAM leaves the encoder's cost as it is (4.8 against 5.0 frames per second, large form) and adds to the decoder and to features the encoder already makes; CascadePSP's two steps at L of 900 run a network of PSPNet size on crops and the page's own time is 6.6 s on a graphics card for a large region; SegFix's smallest backbone is 9.6 million parameters and 16 ms at 2048 by 1024 on a V100; ViTMatte's small form is 25.8 million parameters and needs a trimap. The guided filter needs no weights and no runtime beyond arithmetic: 40 ms per megapixel for one channel and 140 to 150 ms for a colour guide on one core of a desktop processor, whatever the radius (6b, section 4); a 1,024 by 1,024 grid is about 1.05 megapixels. None of the five was timed on the device and the pages give nothing that converts a graphics card's or a desktop processor's figure to a tablet's.
