# How to run the click-prompted models on the photograph's own decode again (12 November 2026)

S is the session's scratch folder, the one the four scripts name at their head. Each script carries the scratch path of the session that wrote it, as the kept scripts of earlier steps carry theirs; point it at the new scratch folder before running it. Every script prints a MADE TO FAIL block and plant lines first, and a run whose plant lines differ from the block is not trusted.

## What must stand first
- Dependencies: `npm ci` in the app, so the bundles can be built from its source.
- The eight photographs, NIR_3461, NIR_3466, NIR_1827, NIR_1651, NIR_3742, NIR_3705, NIR_3710 and NIR_1873, fetched through `node tools/owner-images.mjs --fetch` and through nothing else.
- Each frame rendered once as the editor opens it (the render of step 423) and kept in S/research/whole/frames as `<frame>-open.png` with its `<frame>-open.json`, each 2,800 by 1,864. The render script prints a plant of 137 137 137 for each frame, known to be 137. The four-side-by-side render took about 14 seconds a frame.
- The bundle of the decode and the recipe, S/scratch/step452-bundle.mjs, built from S/scratch/step452-entry.ts by S/scratch/step452-build.mjs. It holds `placeDecode`, `importFile` and the decode.
- The recipe's 22 masks from the decode, by the harness of step 452 (`masks decode <frame>`), in S/research/decode/masks as `decode-<frame>-<subject slug>.png`. The harness is held to the record: NIR_3461 by the decode way must read Sky 73.6, Foliage 25.6 and Built things 35.4 percent at a half.
- S/research/whole holds `samples.json` and the helpers `png.mjs`, `draw.mjs` and `edt.mjs`.
- The runtime: onnxruntime-node 1.31.1, installed in S/rt435 with no install script.
- The four model files, each size equal to the size in `maps-points-notes.md`, from the host us.aws.cdn.hf.co, which the session's network must allow: in S/models435/slimsam, `vision_encoder.onnx` (23,276,014 bytes) and `prompt_encoder_mask_decoder.onnx` (16,557,892 bytes); in S/models435/mobilesam, `mobile_sam_image_encoder.onnx` (28,157,093 bytes) and `sam_mask_decoder_single.onnx` (16,501,323 bytes). SlimSAM's files are from Xenova/slimsam-77-uniform in its folder named onnx, and MobileSAM's from the copy kept by Acly.

## How a script is run
A script is run as a child of S/scratch/step452-runner.mjs. The runner takes only a label, a new one each run, and reads its children from S/scratch/step452-job.json, a list of `{ name, script, args }` written with the Write tool before each run. Each child's output goes to S/progress/step-452-<label>-<name>.txt, and the runner's summary to S/progress/step-452-run-<label>.txt. Both are read with the Read tool. The names say step-452 whatever the step. The runner runs up to four children side by side.

Four SlimSAM runs side by side ran out of memory in the container and the system stopped two of them. SlimSAM runs, and so any run of the models, go at most two at a time; the two that were stopped were run again alone and finished.

## The order
1. `points-decode-inputs.mjs <frame>`, for each of the eight frames. It reads the frame's json for its size, learns the guide's size from a call of `placeDecode` on one pixel, then calls `placeDecode` on a grid the size of the guide (w and h the guide's width and height, k of 1, fromW and fromH the same). It writes `<frame>-colour.png` (RGB from placeDecode's pixels) and `<frame>-grey.png` (three equal channels from its light) to S/inputs2, 1,024 by 682 on these frames. Plant lines: mismatches of the wrong pair 1, of the right pair 0, and resize needed for a guide 2800 by 1864 true. The contract of `placeDecode` that the largest byte of a colour pixel equals that pixel's byte in the grey picture is counted over every pixel and read 0 differing of 698,368 on each frame. Open all sixteen pictures and say what each shows.
2. `points-decode-models.mjs <slim|mobile> <frame>`, for each of the eight frames and both models, sixteen runs. Each runs the model on the frame's colour picture and on its grey picture, one joint prompt for each subject of the first sample set (that subject's sample centres inside, every other name's centres outside), as `step435-maps.mjs` runs them, and writes a mask for each picture and subject to S/masks2, 2,800 by 1,864, one byte a pixel, named like `mobilesam-grey-NIR_3742-water.png` (`mobilesam` or `slimsam`, `colour` or `grey`, the frame, the subject slug). The picture is resized only when its longer side is not 1,024, which on these frames it is. Plant lines: height 682, byte 128, bilinear 0 30, and mismatches 1 and 0. Eighty-eight masks result.
3. `points-decode-compose.mjs <frame>`, for each of the eight frames. It reads the 22 recipe masks and the 88 model masks and writes for each frame-subject a whole picture and an edges picture to S/pictures2, named `whole-<frame>-<subject slug>.png` and `edges-<frame>-<subject slug>.png`, with a per-frame index part. The whole picture has six panels (the frame with its samples, the recipe, the four model masks), each mask a magenta tint by weight with its outline at a half in yellow and a white square with a black rim of one hundredth of the frame. The edges picture has, for each of the five masks, three places along its own edge (one sixth, one half and five sixths of its edge pixels in raster order), each a pair of 240 by 240 crops at the frame's own size, the bare frame and the mask's line one pixel wide, with a white mark four pixels wide. Plant lines: layout whole 1576 and edges 1576, innerEdge counts 8 with places 2,1 3,2 2,3, square side 42 with area over the panel 0.0101 and 228.5 on the frame, and no edge true true false. Open at least three pictures to see every panel and crop drawn, titled and marked.
4. `points-decode-index.mjs`, with no argument. It merges the per-frame index parts into S/pictures2/index.txt and index.json, counts the frame-subjects (22) and counts the files that do not stand. Plant lines: 5 frame-subjects in the planted parts and 1 missing.

## What none of it shows
- The masks are arithmetic in node, the decode's guide and an ONNX run on the plain processor, and say nothing of the device's speed, memory or pictures.
- The backdrop under each mask is the processor's mirror of the photograph as it opens, not the decode itself; a pixel may differ from the device's and a place does not.
- Three places along an edge are a sample of it, and the places follow each mask's own edge, so a word against the recipe compares different places.
