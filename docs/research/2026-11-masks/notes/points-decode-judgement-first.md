# Judgement, first judge: NIR_3461, NIR_3466, NIR_1827 and NIR_1651 (10 frame-subjects)

Every picture named in the index for these ten frame-subjects was opened once, the whole picture and the edges picture, before anything was written. Nothing else was read except the plan, the short page, the section "How the bar was read" and the index. Every size is an estimate by eye against the white square (one hundredth of the frame, about 42 panel pixels across, about 230 frame pixels). Every distance is an estimate by eye against the white L (arms 4 pixels wide). Only the mask cut at a half, as the yellow and red lines show it, is judged. Figures in titles are not used.

Reading used throughout: a lattice pylon or a fence is a thin structure; the sky or grass seen through it is not counted as a region unless the mask treats the whole lattice as one solid shape, which is noted where it happens. NIR_1651 lies turned, sky at the right.

## What this judgement cannot show

- It rests on the opened pictures only. Sizes are estimated by eye against the white square and distances against the white L; the panels draw about 5.5 frame pixels to a panel pixel, so a size near one hundredth, and a distance of 3 to 6 pixels, cannot be told apart reliably. Those cases are marked Uncertain.
- Three places along each edge are a sample. A line that is within 4 at A, B and C can be off elsewhere, and a line that fails at one place may be right along most of its length.
- What a thing is has to be read: whether lattice pylons are solid shapes or open frames, whether a road is a built thing, whether a hills band is foliage. The bar does not say, and different readings move some marks. Each is marked where it bears.
- Thin structures (braces, cables, fence wire) are counted under the region clause by the stated reading, so a mask that gets them wrong is not marked down at the size they actually occupy.
- The masks are made on the processor from the photograph's decode; the backdrop is the processor's copy of the photograph as it opens. Nothing here says how a model would run on the device or how the picture looks on the device.
- The two judges split the eight photographs between them, so nothing here compares the two judges' reading of the same frame.
- No figure from the pictures' titles was used, so nothing here says how many pixels a mask holds.
