## The one place the decode way is filled
S/step452-harness.mjs, the function `countDecode(frame)`, marked THE ONE PLACE STEP 453 FILLS. It is the only function of the harness that throws until filled, and everything else (labelling, fitting, weighting, rendering, carrying, writing) is shared by both ways.
- It takes a frame name and gives { way: 'decode', W, H, k, w, h, n, cols, rows, light, cues, cueNames }, the shape `countDrawn` gives: W and H the frame's own size (2,800 by 1,864), k the thinning step (the page's rule, every k-th pixel of every k-th row, k from the 1.2e6 cap, which is 3 here), w and h the counted grid, n their product, cols and rows each counted pixel's place in the frame, light one byte a pixel for the whole frame (what the edge stage follows), cues the ten Float32Array(n) in the order of cueNames (the eight cues of src/placecues.ts then the two of src/placestructure.ts).
- It is filled from step 451's report: one call of the new function of src/placedecode.ts as its contract (copied whole in progress/step-451-report.txt) gives it. The frame's file is reached through resolve() of tools/owner-images.mjs and decoded by the app's own calls as S/step423-render.mjs does as far as the decode; no balance, exposure, swap, denoise or look is applied.
- S/step452-entry.ts gains, beside the marked comment, the exports the filled function needs (the new module's function, and importFile, decode and the like from the pattern of S/step423-entry.ts), each by path. The bundle is then built again (S/step452-build.mjs) and a plant is read: the decode way's light and cues must be of the frame's own size.
- The step may make nothing else in the harness differ; a cue it cannot count from the decode is EJECT naming the cue.

## The order step 453 runs them
1. Fill `countDecode` and the entry; `node --check` on the harness; build the bundle (one child, S/step452-build.mjs).
2. `masks decode <frame>` for each of the eight frames, four children side by side per label (two labels). Each prints its names and counts and writes D/masks/decode-<frame>-<subject slug>.png for each subject the frame's samples name: twenty-two masks in all.
3. `copy drawn <first> <second>` and `copy decode <first> <second>` for the pairs NIR_3705 to NIR_3710, NIR_3461 to NIR_3466 and NIR_3742 to NIR_1873: six children, four side by side. (The files for NIR_3705 to NIR_3710 by the drawn way already stand from the trial and are made again by the same command with the same result.)
4. `node D/index-md.mjs` (no arguments): merges the per-run index files into D/masks/index.json and D/copies/index.json, and writes D/index.md with every picture still to be composed marked MISSING. It is run again at the end.
5. `node D/compose-comparison.mjs <frame> <subject> D/pictures/comparison-<frame>-<subject slug>.png` for each of the twenty-two frame-subjects (no fourth word: the fourth word `standin` is only for trials). Four side by side. The subject is as the samples file spells it (Sky, Foliage, Built things, Water); the slug is the lower case with hyphens.
6. `node D/compose-copied.mjs <first> <second> D/pictures/copied-<first>-to-<second>.png` for the three pairs.
7. `node D/index-md.mjs` again, and open three pictures, one of them a copied-recipe picture, to see that every panel and crop is drawn, titled and marked.

## What each picture holds
- A comparison picture, 1,576 by 1,267 pixels: the frame with its samples; the mask from the drawn picture; the mask from the decode, each drawn over the frame as the sheet maker draws it (magenta tint by weight, outline at a half in yellow) under a title naming which it is and the share of the frame taken at a half; under them three places (A, B, C, outlined on the three panels above) each a row of three crops of 240 by 240 pixels at the frame's own size: the bare frame, the drawn way's edge, the decode way's edge, each edge a line one pixel wide, each crop with a white L, each arm exactly four pixels wide with a black rim, at its bottom left.
- The three places are spaced evenly by the count of edge pixels in raster order along the edge of the mask from the decode cut at a half (one sixth, one half and five sixths of the way), each moved to lie inside the frame and moved on to the next edge pixel where it would overlap an earlier place; along the drawn way's edge instead where the decode mask has none; evenly across the frame's middle where neither has an edge, and the row label says so.
- A copied-recipe picture, 1,590 pixels wide and 286 tall for each subject plus 28: a row for each subject both frames name; the second frame with its samples, the first frame's recipe applied to the second by the drawn way, the same by the decode way, the second frame's own mask by the decode way.

## What none of it shows
- The top panels bring the frame down to 512 pixels wide (385 for the copied picture), so a band of a few pixels is the crops' to show, and the crops hold three places of the frame only.
- Raster order is not length along a contour: the three places are even by count of edge pixels, so a long contour near the top of the frame can hold two of them and a place can lie on a speck. The judging steps read the places as places, and say where a mask's worst edge is when it is not among them.
- The drawn way's masks are those of 11 November: made from the picture as it opens, with the editor's measured denoise and the CPU mirror of the edit in sRGB; the device draws through its own graphics chip, so a pixel may differ from the device's and a place does not.
- A trial picture in D/trial carries STAND-IN on its face; it shows the layout and nothing about a mask from the decode.
