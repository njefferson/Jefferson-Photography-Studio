# 103 · An infrared white reads inside the scale with no profile loaded

## Context

Record 102 reads the Temperature and Tint through the profile in force: the one the file embeds, else one the reader has loaded for the camera, else the camera's stock matrix. With no profile loaded, an infrared white from this camera sits at the cool end of the Temperature thumb or past it. Of 37 data-found whites (32 of the shared raws, four more raws and the keep file's photograph) none reads inside Adobe's 2000 K to 50000 K through the stock matrix. They read 1666.7 K to 1733.9 K, five of them cooler than the SDK table's last row and so held at 1666.7 K with the rest carried in the tint. The thumb rests at its cool end on all 37, a drag toward cooler moves nothing on all 37, and typing back the whole numbers the box shows misses the photograph's own balance by up to 1.1 percent.

The capability this record serves: with no profile loaded, a raw's infrared white sits inside the scale, the box takes any number and lands on it, and a drag moves the white both ways. Lightroom has that capability only when the camera profile is the author's infrared one; record 102 gives the app the same through a profile the file embeds or the reader loads. This record asks what the app can do for itself when none is loaded, and it was written after the candidates were measured on 29 October 2026 (IR-SCIENCE.md section 3, the block of that date). Record 102's Context points here.

## Looked up

- **What the author's two profile files change, read with the app's own reader and written here as arithmetic only.** Each file's daylight (6500 K) colour matrix is the camera's stock matrix with its three XYZ columns multiplied by three constants (the worst entry miss over the largest entry is 0.00002 for both files): the middle constant is the unit, the X constant is above it and the Z constant below it by equal amounts, and that amount is twice as large in the -100 file as in the -50 file. The 2850 K matrix of each file is not a scaling of the stock matrix, and the app holds no 2850 K matrix for the camera. So reading a camera neutral through a file divides the stock reading's X by the larger constant and its Z by the smaller, which moves the white toward blue: across the 32 shared raws the -100 file moves every white by 465 to 503 mired (median 493) and its tint by +96 to +127, the -50 file by 261 to 294 mired (median 283) and +29 to +35, and the spread of the 32 whites in mired grows from 23.0 to 59.2 and 55.6. The -100 file calls daylight a camera neutral much nearer to where these whites sit than the stock matrix does (the largest log-ratio from the raws' median gains is 0.33, against 0.95 for the stock matrix and 0.72 for the -50 file), which is why its readings come out near 10000 K. The profiles are the author's work and are not carried.
- **darktable**, temperature.c (2233 lines, read whole) and illuminants.h (587 lines, read whole), raw GitHub host, 29 October. Range: 1901 K to 25000 K, tint 0.135 to 2.326, channel coefficients 0 to 8. Method: Planck's law summed against the CIE 1931 observer below 4000 K, the CIE daylight series above; the tint is a division of Y that the file's own comments call not orthogonal to the locus. Below its lowest row: the requested temperature is clamped to 1901 K and the inverse clamps its answer, so a cooler white reads 1901 K and its place survives only in the coefficients. Its colour calibration's locus is a spline fit valid from 1667 K, where the SDK's table stops.
- **RawTherapee**, colortemp.h (647 lines, read whole) and colortemp.cc (459371 bytes; the code read line by line, the numeric spectral tables searched and not paged). Range: 1500 K to 60000 K. Method: the white is computed directly from Planck's law at or below 4000 K, with no isotherm table, and clamped at 1500 K; Robertson's 31-row table appears only in a function documented as valid from 1666.7 K, and its copy prints u of 0.24792 at 325 mired where Adobe's SDK prints 0.24702.
- **Adobe's DNG SDK 1.4** (the saved whole copies, read again at the two directions below 1667 K): Set_xy_coord holds a cooler white at 1666.7 K and carries the rest in the tint; Get_xy_coord extrapolates the last two rows in a straight line, so the two directions are not mirror images.
- **The 25 October build's locus files** (kept in a worktree, not applied): Planck's law summed against the CIE 1931 observer from 380 to 780 nm at 5 nm, rows from 625 to 2000 mired at 25-mired spacing, each carrying an oriented normal.
- **Not read:** the spectral data tables of RawTherapee's colortemp.cc; Adobe's own SDK copy; what Lightroom shows for a white cooler than its own table.
