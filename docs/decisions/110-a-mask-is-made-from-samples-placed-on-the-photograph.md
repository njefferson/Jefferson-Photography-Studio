# 110 · A mask is made from samples placed on the photograph

## Context

**The goal.** A mask is made in the editor from named samples placed on the photograph, and it takes what the samples hold. The mask copies to other photographs, and on each of them what the copy would take is shown before anything is applied. A copy that takes the wrong thing is put right by samples placed on that photograph. This end result was stated before this record and before any of the research it rests on.

Where it stands. The test page carries record 109's samples, on staging as 2.78: named circles placed on a photograph, whose counted pixels (one pixel in nine of the drawn frame, every third column of every third row) become labels, with the pixels no sample holds named rest. A count gives nine cues at each counted pixel when the labels come from samples (lightness, saturation, red against green, red against blue, green against blue, texture at 5 and at 15, edge alignment at 15 and at 45; height is left out), each binned in 64 bins on a fixed scale. It prints, for each two names, the distance on each cue and on the best pair of cues; it fits for each name a range of whole bins of one cue, alone or joined with a second range by Only where both or Subtract, which takes a counted pixel or does not; it keeps those ranges on the device under the photograph's name and reads them on the next photograph. The editor's masks are six kinds by number, radial, gradient, brush, colour, sky and rectangle (`MaskLayer` in `src/pipeline.ts`). The brush and the sky are one-channel bitmaps of 0 to 255, at most eight drawn at once in one atlas whose size is the first bitmap's (`src/gl.ts`). A saved mask goes to another photograph as its numbers, a Sky mask detected again there with its hand strokes replayed and a brush refused in words, and it is added with nothing shown first (`applySavedMask` in `src/main.ts`; `canTravel` in `src/maskstore.ts`). The editor holds no cue code: `src/placecues.ts` is imported by the test page alone.

What the device showed. Seven presses of the test page at 2.78 on one iPad on 10 November 2026, between 18:21 and 19:44 California time. Every share below is a share of sampled pixels on one photograph, never of the photograph.

- Every press counted 579,393 pixels, and the samples held 7.0 to 17.4 percent of them, from 40,579 in the second press to 100,905 in the fourth. What a range takes among the other 82.6 to 93.0 percent is in no figure: the count drew pictures for two names at most, and in presses 2 to 7 only selections kept from other photographs, none fitted on the photograph pressed.
- On its own photograph a fitted range took most of its own name: the sky .991 to 1.000 in five presses and .932 in the sixth, foliage .853 to .998, built things .539 to .917, water .966 and .775. Of the twenty single ranges fitted, three held a target share under .45 (.086, .425 and .103), each against names that were placed beside it.
- The cue a range chose for one name changed from photograph to photograph: for the sky texture at 15, texture at 5 or red against blue; for foliage red against blue, texture at 15, texture at 5 or lightness; for built things edge alignment at 45, lightness or texture at 15.
- With the app's own sky selection taken out of the count, the sky's range took 0.000 in four presses and .018 in a fifth, so on those photographs it said little of the sky beyond what that selection already holds.
- Carried to another photograph, a range held within .03 of that photograph's own fit for the sky and foliage between three pairs taken as one scene by their file numbers alone, and did not hold between scenes: the sky's texture ranges took from .005 to .978 of the sky, foliage ranges took built things with them in four readings of one source, and built things' ranges took the sky or the foliage, one of them at target shares of .012 to .544.
- At half and at a quarter of each sample's radius the best pair's distance rose by .029 to .148 where it was under .95 at the full radius, while the counted pixels fell to about a quarter and a sixteenth; the report fitted no range at the smaller radii.
- A count took 1,016 to 1,423 ms with its report and pair figures, the labelling alone 62 to 168 ms; the app's own sky selection was built in 529 to 770 ms; the picture code was built in the first press of each page loading and kept in the other five.

What decides it. Three findings of those figures and the pages. A range reads one or two of nine cues and gives 0 or 1, where the two segmentation programs read, ilastik and Weka, read every feature they are given and give a probability. Whether a carried range holds is not known until it is read on the photograph it lands on, which is what the goal asks to be shown before anything applies. And a range keeps no counts, so samples placed on a second photograph cannot be added to what it was fitted from. The work has two stages: the mask on the test page first, to be built as 2.79, and the editor's mask kind after it, by the plan that follows; the rendering the editor takes is picked from the pictures the device draws of the renderings below.

- **Wikipedia, Naive Bayes classifier.** A prior for each class and, for each feature on its own, a distribution given the class, normal, binned or a kernel density; the posterior is the prior times the product of the feature likelihoods, scaled; fitted by a closed-form count with no iteration, with a pseudocount of one for counts that never occur; a small training set is enough. - **Wikipedia, Random forest.** Many trees, each on a bootstrap sample, a random subset of the features at each split, a few hundred to several thousand trees, a vote; no timing.
- **The darktable development manual (5.6), masking and blending, and its page on mask refinement and additional controls.** Feathering aligns a mask's edges with edges in a guide image, works best where the mask already roughly follows an edge and overshoots at a large radius; a Gaussian blur comes after it; mask opacity and contrast are two further controls; its example sets feathering 50, blur 5, opacity 0.3 and contrast 0.5. ## Rejected

- **The range as the mask.** It gives 0 or 1 at every pixel, reads one or two of nine cues, chose a different cue for one name from photograph to photograph, held a target share under .45 in three of twenty fits, and between scenes its sky ranges took from .005 to .978 of the sky. It keeps no counts, so samples on a second photograph cannot be added to it. Its rows stay on the page beside the recipe, as the measure the recipe is read against.
- **A joint histogram on the best pair.** It reads two cues, and the best pair differed from one pair of names and one photograph to the next on the device (lightness with texture at 15, texture at 15 with edge alignment at 45, red against blue with texture at 15 and others), so a recipe on one pair carries the range's defect at one remove.
- **A random forest.** No page read gives its cost or its size; a fit grows trees by splitting and not by one count, hundreds of trees are stored node by node, and its counts do not add. Its generalisation is ilastik's statement and nothing here measured it.
- **A mask solved on each photograph from its own marks.** The random walker and GrabCut solve one picture from its own seeds or box and carry nothing, so a copy could show nothing on a photograph until it was marked there, which the goal's showing before applying cannot meet. The random walker is a sparse solve over every counted pixel with no cost on its page; GrabCut gives a hard label.
- **One colour distance from an averaged reference.** Record 109 rejected it for the check because it drops the sample's spread; as a mask it reads colour alone, where the device's ranges chose texture, lightness and edge alignment for most names.
- **Counts with no pseudocount.** A bin one side never filled would weigh 0 or 1 whatever the other eight cues say, which is the case the naive Bayes page's pseudocount exists for.
- **A pseudocount of one on counts left unbalanced.** A pixel the samples say nothing of would lean, on every empty cue, toward the side with fewer pixels by the ratio of the two totals each plus 64, and over nine cues it would be taken by whichever name was sampled least.
- **Priors from each side's share of the sampled pixels.** That share measures how many samples were placed, not the photograph; equal priors are Weka's balance and give exactly a half where there is no evidence.
- **Leaving out red against blue because the other two differences fix it.** In this form each cue's histogram is its own one-dimensional view, and two of the twenty single ranges on the device chose it. Counting it beside the other two adds to the form's overconfidence, which the Weight rendering drawn beside the cut shows.
- **A fourth rendering, softened by a box mean of the cut.** A blur that does not follow the picture's edges spreads the mask across them, which 042 counts as a coarse selection, and the editor's Feather already softens the radial, rectangle, colour and sky kinds.
- **Refining the weight rather than the cut.** Inside a window where p is constant the guided filter gives p's mean whatever the guide holds, so a graded weight's own gradations, and not the picture's edges, would set where the output moves; the editor's refinement cuts at a half first.
- **A separate mark for what to leave out.** Every placed name already serves as the others, and what any fit takes in rest is unmeasured; the device's pictures over the whole photograph are what would show the need.
- **A smaller default sample.** The rise of the pair figures at smaller radii is not separated from the count's own part, and nothing was fitted there.
- **Adding the source's counts to the redo.** Where a copy took the wrong thing, its counts are what took it, and adding them back carries the error into the fresh fit.
- **Riding the brush kind.** It cannot travel while `canTravel` refuses a brush, its keep file stores its bytes, and the brush's controls and paint tools would act on it.
- **Riding the sky kind.** Every test of type 4 would take it for the sky, its status line would say a sky was detected, and the sky's Reach and colour switches would act on a generator it does not use; separating them needs a field read at every such test, and a test that missed it would act silently as the sky.
- **Weighing the recipe in the shader at each pixel.** It needs cue values the editor's pictures do not hold.
- **Storing the map's bytes in the keep file.** A copy has to make the map again on the photograph it lands on whatever is stored, so making it again at open as well keeps one route.
