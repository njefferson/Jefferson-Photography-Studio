# 109 · The test page names regions by samples placed on the photograph

## Context

**The goal.** On the test page a region of the photograph is marked by a sample placed on the picture, wider than a few pixels and of a size that is set, moved over the area and confirmed, with a magnified view above the finger on a touch screen, in whichever form the field's convention gives; each sample is named for what it holds; the check then measures what tells the named regions apart. With it, several samples can share a name and a kind nobody marks is absent, and the check counts every cue and pair of cues between each two names, fits selections, reads them on the next photograph and says when a sample holds too few pixels to measure. Nothing of the editor changes. This end result was stated on 9 November 2026, before any work toward it.

Where it stands. The Separation check is on record 106's test page, on staging as 2.77. A press draws one photograph, the one handed to the page through record 108's door or else the practice photograph, at the balance the editor opens at, and counts one pixel in nine of the drawn frame. Its places part, built on 9 November, labels the counted pixels of three photographs of one marina by polygons read off pictures by eye (sky, conifers, broadleaf trees, snags, shore, water, docks and boats); any other photograph is labelled by the check's own four labels. For every two labels it counts eight cues (lightness, saturation, red against green, red against blue, green against blue, texture at 5, texture at 15 and height), prints the Hellinger distance between the two labels' histograms on each cue and the best pair of cues together, fits a selection of whole bins for each target by the app's two joins, draws what each selection takes, keeps it in the browser's storage under the photograph's name and reads it on the next photograph opened. A label with fewer than 50 counted pixels takes no part. Five presses were made on a desktop on 9 November; every figure was checked against the page's own reports, and the two photographs pressed twice repeated in every pair figure. Each press took 49.8 to 53.2 seconds, 44.0 to 46.0 of them in building the picture code again.

What decides it. Three findings of those figures, which the science file's joined account of 9 November and its passage of 10 November carry. First, no counted cue but height parts the conifers from the built things (at most 0.473 against the docks and 0.418 against the boats) or the docks from the boats (at most 0.446): the two textures count how much the lightness varies and do not part them, and whether that variation has one direction is not counted. Second, height is the strongest cue where it is measured and does not carry from one photograph to the next as the framing moves from 77 to 70 to 64 mm. Third, the drawn places exist for three photographs of one scene, so on any other photograph the check has only its own four labels. A way for the person at the device to name regions on any photograph, and cues of direction counted beside the eight, answer the three. The samples are the test page's own marks, made on the page and kept on the device by the page; they are not the reader's edit, and the page still writes nothing back to the photograph.

   - **The structure cues: two, both the structure tensor's coherence, named edge alignment at 15 and edge alignment at 45.** Each is counted from the frame's lightness alone, the one byte for each pixel of the whole frame that the textures use, at each counted pixel:
     - The derivatives at a pixel of the whole frame are the centred differences, the lightness one pixel to the right less the lightness one pixel to the left, and the lightness one pixel below less the lightness one pixel above, where a neighbour past the frame's edge is taken as the nearest pixel inside it (the HOG page's centred masks; the tensor page's finite differences).
     - The window is a square of 15 pixels, half-width 7, for the first, and of 45 pixels, half-width 22, for the second, centred on the counted pixel's place in the whole frame and clipped at the frame's edge, every pixel inside it weighted alike, as the textures' windows are. Its three sums are the sum of the squared horizontal derivative, the sum of the squared vertical derivative, and the sum of their product, each taken exactly.
     - The value is the square of the difference of the first two sums plus four times the square of the third, all over the square of the sum of the first two; it is 0 where the sum of the first two is 0, that is, where the window's lightness is constant. It lies from 0 to 1, since the third sum's square never exceeds the product of the first two; it is 1 where every derivative in the window lies along one line and falls toward 0 as their directions spread. It equals the tensor page's coherence, with weights all alike in place of a Gaussian.
     - Each is binned in 64 bins on the scale from 0 to 1, so a range fitted on one photograph means the same on another.
     - Why these windows. The 15 is the wider texture's window, so the amount of variation and its direction are counted over the same pixels. The 45 is the textures' own ratio of three carried one step up: an edge that runs straight across a longer stretch keeps the value high there, where a window of short edges turning every way does not. That is this record's reading of the definition and the nearest a window comes to straightness, which no page read defines; many short parallel edges read the same as one long straight one. The tensor page's own words are the trade: the wider window is steadier against noise and coarser in place.

Where the lightness changes by a step or so of the byte, as the sky's wider texture measured on the device implies it does there, the value rests on a handful of tiny differences. On a sample of the default radius about 18 percent of its counted pixels have a 15-pixel window reaching past its edge and about half have a 45-pixel window doing so, so a sample is placed well inside its region. For a frame of independent noise the value is of the order of 2 over the count of pixels in the window, about 0.01 at 15 and 0.001 at 45, this record's arithmetic and no page's. Nothing is known of either on these photographs until the device counts them.

## Rejected

- **A drag whose extent is the area.** The drag then places and sizes at once, so it needs every path that is not a drag besides it, and the size cannot be changed afterwards without drawing again; on a touch screen the finger covers the very area being drawn out; and the shapes those programs drag are rectangles, whose count depends on two numbers where the circle's depends on one.
- **A single point.** A point gives one value and no spread, and the check compares the spread of each cue over each name; the goal asks for a sample wider than a few pixels.
- **Several samples feeding one range.** In Adobe's colour range the samples join into one range of colour; here several samples of one name join as places and the check fits its own ranges on every cue. The modifier and the click that add and take away a sample have no counterpart on a touch screen.
- **RawTherapee's reference circle inside a separate area.** It makes a sample two shapes, and its selection is one distance from an averaged reference, where the check's form is a spread of values for each cue compared by histograms; the sample's own spread is the information the check uses.
- **A brush.** A stroke is a path gesture, the hardest under tremor and one that needs a path of single points beside it; painting a region by hand is what the polygons read by eye already were, made on a picture rather than on the device.
- **The drawn places as the only labels.** They exist for three photographs of one scene; on any other photograph the check has only its own four labels, and drawing polygons for each new photograph by eye is not done on the device.
- **A magnified view brought up by a press held for a time,** as a touch screen's own text magnifier is: a held press on a short window is a timed gesture the doctrine's section refuses.
- **Snapping the marker to the photograph's edges.** It would put a sample on the boundary between two regions.
- **An overlap given to the name placed last, or counted under both names.** The first makes a count depend on the order of placing; the second adds the same pixels to both names and draws their pair figures together.
- **Height kept as a cue for samples.** It would measure where the samples were put.
- **Foerstner's q as a second cue.** It is 1 less the coherence, so its histograms mirror the coherence's and it adds nothing.
- **The eigenvalues themselves, Harris's response, Shi-Tomasi's value and Foerstner's w.** Each grows with the square of the lightness differences and has no fixed range, so a fixed scale for its bins would have to be invented, and a range fitted on one photograph would not mean the same on the next.
- **The histogram of oriented gradients.** It gives a histogram for each cell, and reducing it to one value for each pixel needs a rule no page read gives.
- **The shape index.** It is made from the Hessian rather than the gradient, runs from minus 1 to 1 and is undefined in flat regions, which are much of a sky.
- **The share of the coherence along the frame's own axes,** which the same three sums give. It is this record's algebra and no page's, and like height it depends on how the camera was held, which differs from one photograph to the next.
- **A Gaussian window in place of the square.** The test page makes its windows by running sums, which a square allows and a Gaussian does not exactly; the tensor page's caution on a box is for its form with two scales, and the textures' windows are squares.
- **A window of 5 pixels.** Twenty-five derivatives give a value that rests on too few to say much of direction; the 15 shares the wider texture's pixels.
- **darktable's details threshold and RawPedia's Structure mask.** The first is taken from the raw data before demosaicing, where the test page has the drawn lightness; the second is given with no formula.
- **A measure of straightness along an edge.** No page read defines one, and the page that would, the line segment detector's, was not read.
