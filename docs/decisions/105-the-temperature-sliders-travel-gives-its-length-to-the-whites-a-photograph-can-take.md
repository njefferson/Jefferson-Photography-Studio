# 105 · The Temperature slider's travel gives its length to the whites a photograph can take

## Context

**The goal.** The slider's travel is usable, with the whites a photograph can take spread over most of it and neither end spent on a pure blue or on what lies past red.

Where it stands. The Temperature thumb is one step to a mired, 1842 steps long, from 537 K at the left to 50000 K at the right (`TEMPERATURE_MIRED_FAR` and `TEMPERATURE_MIRED_NEAR` in `src/balancestore.ts`, the minus 1862 and minus 20 of `ir.html`, held together by `tools/join-fold-check.mjs`). Record 102 gave it Adobe's ends, 2000 K to 50000 K, which are 480 steps. Record 103 lowered the cool end to the lowest white the camera's gains can express, 537 K, so that a typed 600 K lands, and the same constant set the thumb's own end. The thumb grew from 480 steps to 1842 and no record weighed what that does to the share of it a photograph's white can use.

What the 1842 steps are spent on, from step 264's figures recomputed by a runner in step 270. Adobe's whole scale is 26.1 percent of the travel; 73.9 percent lies cooler than 2000 K and 46.8 percent cooler than 1000 K, where a typed white gives the flat, clipped green that record 103's outcome describes. A Z 50 photograph's whites take 3.2 percent of the travel under Infrared Temp -100 (7414 K to 13211 K, 59.2 steps), 3.0 percent under Infrared Temp -50 (2948 K to 3526 K, 55.6 steps) and 1.6 percent with no profile loaded (1651 K to 1734 K, 29.0 steps). Three percent of a travel that fits a phone's width is a handful of pixels, which a fingertip cannot place (arithmetic on an assumed length of a few hundred pixels, not a measurement on a device; the device pass reads it), so a drag cannot place a white inside its own band; a typed number can, and is exact. The other end is cheap: whites bluer than 20000 K take 1.6 percent.

What the record asks: how the travel should be spaced and ended so that the whites a photograph can take have most of it, without choosing a range from the data (record 102's boundary) and without undoing what records 102, 103 and 104 shipped. The finding that decides it is arithmetic, not taste: the three bands sit 530 mired apart (75.7 to 134.9, 283.6 to 339.2 and 576.7 to 605.7) and each is 29 to 59 mired wide, so no single fixed travel that covers all three can give any of them most of its length. The nine spacings measured (Measured below) put one band at up to 25.1 percent and the others under 4 percent or outside the travel. Only a control whose travel follows the white can reach most of it.

## Looked up

Step 264 read the sources for this record and step 270 added the arithmetic; the sources, by what each said:

- **RawTherapee**, `rtgui/tools/whitebalance.cc` on the dev branch (source read). The temperature adjuster runs 1500 K to 60000 K with its centre at 4750 K, even in kelvin below the centre and on a fourth-power curve above it; the tint adjuster is logarithmic and the temperature adjuster is not. Its maintainer wrote, on an issue about infrared, that the temperature slider is non-linear.
- **darktable**, `src/iop/temperature.c` on the master branch (source read, as the fetch tool reported it). The kelvin slider is even in kelvin from 1901 K to 25000 K; the slider's default and argument order were inferred by the fetch tool from the call.
- **Lightroom and Camera Raw.** How the Temperature thumb is spaced is stated by no source read. The Adobe help page answered the fetch tool with 403. Forum and controller pages give the scale's ends (2000 to 50000) and call the kelvin figures approximate; one controller plug-in maps its 128 steps linearly across the range by its own choice, which says nothing of the on-screen thumb. A search summary of a second plug-in issue (not opened) says Temperature is clamped to 2000 K to 8000 K there.
- **The mired scale** (Wikipedia's page on the mired, read). Priest observed in 1932 that the just noticeable difference between illuminants "is directly related to the difference of the reciprocals of their temperatures", so the mired scale "is a better measure of perceptual color difference than the temperature scale". This is the one outside support for the app's spacing; none of the three programs read spaces its thumb in mired.
- **Adobe's schema** (record 102): Temperature 2000 to 50000, Tint minus 150 to 150, ranges only; it says nothing of how a control is spaced.
- **The arithmetic**, by a runner written for step 270 and made to fail first: its control lines read 73.9, 3.2, 59.2, 500.0, 20.0 and 46.8 as known before any figure was taken. Measured below.
- **Not read:** how Lightroom spaces its thumb; Adobe's help page; RawPedia (404 on two paths); any study of how fine a finger can place a thumb on a tablet slider. The widths of the bands are from the 32 shared raws (record 102) and the 37 whites of record 103; a photograph from another body or another filter may sit elsewhere.

## Options

1. **A coarse and a fine control, with the coarse thumb's cool end at 1500 K. Chosen.** The coarse thumb stays one step to a mired, which keeps the unit the SDK works in and the one outside support there is, and keeps Adobe's warm end, so the keep file's hand-set white at about 45975 K still sits inside it. Its cool end moves from 537 K to 1500 K (667 mired, 647 steps), a figure RawTherapee's source and record 103's first option both name; the typed box keeps its floor of 537 K, so a typed 600 K still lands and the thumb rests at its end for it, as record 102 already has it for any white outside an end. That ends the 46.8 percent of the travel spent below 1000 K and takes the three bands to 9.2, 8.6 and 4.5 percent (option C of Measured). The fine control is a second thumb on the same row whose whole travel is a fixed number of mired either side of the white where the reader last set it, one step to a mired, so a drag that was a fingertip across 29 steps is a hand's width across the fine travel: at plus and minus 45 mired the three bands take 66, 62 and 32 percent (option J). It moves the same three gains through `moveBalance`, so nothing new is stored, and it is one undo step per gesture like the coarse thumb. What it costs: a second control on the Basic tab's Temperature row, which owes the accessibility walk, the control walk's inventory, the 44-pixel target and a name that says what it does; a rule for where its centre stands (where the reader last set the white by the coarse thumb, the box, a tap, a profile switch or the double tap, and not where the photograph's data put it, so it chooses no range from the data); a span chosen by the build plan from the band widths measured here and confirmed on a device, which is a number the app picks and must say so in words; and the trimmed cool end is the first part to build because it is a change of two constants and their check. The fine control is the only one of the nine designs measured that gives a band most of its length, which is the goal's first half; the trimmed end alone is its second half.
2. **The trimmed end alone: one step to a mired, 1500 K to 50000 K. Kept.** The smallest change and the first half of option 1. The bands take 9.2, 8.6 and 4.5 percent of 647 steps, about three times the share today and under a tenth of a travel that still has to hold every white. It does not reach most of the travel and it is kept as the fall-back should the fine control cost more on a device than it earns.
3. **The same trim with the warm end at 25000 K** (627 steps, bands 9.4, 8.9 and 4.6 percent). Kept as a variant, not chosen: it takes the share bluer than 20000 K from 4.6 to 1.6 percent and gains 0.2 of a point for a band, and it puts the keep file's hand-set white at 45975 K outside the travel, where record 103 measured it staying inside.
4. **Leave the travel as it is, with the typed box as the route.** Kept as the standing position. The box is exact and needs nothing; a finger gets 3 percent. It meets none of the goal.

## Rejected

- **A different spacing in one thumb: even in log kelvin, even in kelvin, darktable's ends, RawTherapee's shape** (options E to I of Measured). None gives all three bands more than 12.7 percent; the best for one band is darktable's 1901 K to 25000 K, 25.1 percent for the -100 band, at 2.5 percent for the -50 band and with the no-profile whites outside the travel, the failure record 103 closed; RawTherapee's shape gives 9.7, 8.9 and 1.3, no better than the trimmed mired thumb and worse for the no-profile case; even in kelvin leaves 60.7 percent on whites bluer than 20000 K. A spacing that is not the mired also drops the one outside support the app's unit has, and brings in another program's centre (4750 K) as if it were this app's.
- **Mired between Adobe's own ends, 2000 K to 50000 K** (option B). It is the scale of record 102 exactly and gives 12.3 and 11.6 percent under the two profiles, but it puts every no-profile white outside the travel, so the thumb rests at an end for all 37 and a drag toward cooler moves nothing: the failure record 103 was written to close, brought back.
- **Ends that follow the profile in force, or the photograph's found white.** It would give a band most of the travel and is the same thing as a range read off the data, which record 102 rejected ("a range, a sign or a default chosen by the app") and record 103 rejected again ("a range read off the raws"). A profile switch would also re-scale the thumb under the finger.
- **A logarithmic spacing alone as the fix.** Same as the first entry of this list for the no-profile band (1.1 to 1.4 percent) and it spends 13.7 to 26.1 percent of the travel on the blue end.
- **Fixing the fine control's span in this record.** The bands' widths are measured and the span is a ratio of them to a travel; a thumb's feel under a finger is not measured in a container, and a number fixed here would be a figure from software drawing.

The queue's test for a dependency is whether work ranked below would have to be redone once this lands, and it is not: nothing below reads the Temperature thumb; the look records read the gains and the opening balance, never the thumb's travel, and the capability items below read neither. It is not first, and being reported does not move it: it is a usability defect of a control shipped on 31 October, and a reported defect of this kind goes where the queue's own order puts it, below the picture and launch defects already ranked (069, 071, 052, 073, 013) and above 068, the first capability item. Two things argue against moving it higher. The rank is the defect's natural place, not the dependency's: no dependency privileges it.
