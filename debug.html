Measure a lens

An infrared-converted lens sends a little more infrared straight up the middle than it does to the corners, so the centre of every frame comes out brighter and a slightly different colour. This measures yours, from your own photographs, on this device.

What to give it: frames of nothing — clear blue sky, filling the whole frame, no sun in shot, no clouds, no lens hood, well exposed but nothing blown. Four or five at each focal length you actually use. RAW or JPEG, both work; the RAW is better. Shoot them the way you shoot, and take them at a few focal lengths across the zoom — the hot-spot changes with focal length and aperture.

Give it the whole set at once. Select as many as you like, and a .zip counts as everything inside it — which is how a folder of RAW files travels on an iPad, since zipping is the only way iOS hands over a NEF without turning it into a JPEG first. Frames are read one at a time, so the size of the zip does not matter. They are sorted into lenses and focal lengths for you; you do not have to keep them apart. A long run can be stopped and keeps what it has measured so far.

Nothing is uploaded and nothing leaves this device. What comes out is a small block of numbers you can copy or save — a few kilobytes of text, not the photographs.
